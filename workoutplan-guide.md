This transition from high-volume running to a low-impact home routine is a common and necessary shift. At 5'10" and 155 lbs, you are already at a lean weight. Your goal isn't just "weight loss" in the traditional sense; it is **body recomposition**. You want to strip the remaining fat while adding density to your chest and abs to create that athletic look.

Since you are used to the endorphin rush of 10-mile runs, we need a plan that keeps your heart rate high (metabolic conditioning) without pounding your knee joints.

### **The "Knee-Preservation" Strategy**

* **Eliminate Impact:** No jumping jacks, box jumps, or burpees.
* **Posterior Chain Focus:** We will emphasize glutes and hamstrings (back of the legs) to support the knee, rather than quad-dominant moves that shear the knee joint.
* **Time Under Tension:** Since we have minimal weights, we will use slower tempos to make light weights feel heavy.

---

### **Weekly Schedule**

This is a 6-day active cycle. Since you requested a daily plan, we will alternate intensity to allow recovery.

* **Monday:** Upper Body Push (Chest Focus) + Core
* **Tuesday:** Lower Body (Knee-Friendly) + Low Impact Cardio
* **Wednesday:** Upper Body Pull (Back & Biceps) + Core
* **Thursday:** Low Impact HIIT (Sweat Session)
* **Friday:** Full Body Volume (Circuit)
* **Saturday:** Active Recovery / Mobility
* **Sunday:** Rest or Long Walk

---

### **The Workouts**

#### **Monday: Chest & Triceps (Push) + Abs**

*Focus: Hypertrophy (Muscle Growth) for the chest.*
*Perform as a circuit: 3 rounds, 45 seconds work, 15 seconds rest.*

1. **Push-ups (Slow Tempo):** 3 seconds down, 1 second up. If too easy, elevate your feet on a chair.
2. **Dumbbell Floor Press:** Lie on your back, press weights up. Squeeze chest at the top.
3. **Dumbbell Floor Flys:** Lie on back, slight bend in elbows, open arms wide until elbows touch floor, bear hug back to center.
4. **Overhead Tricep Extension:** Standing or seated (to protect back), hold one dumbbell with both hands behind head and extend up.
5. **Plank to Push-up:** Start in plank, push up to hand support, lower back down.
6. **Dead Bugs (Core):** Lie on back, arms and knees up. Lower opposite arm and leg slowly. Keep lower back glued to the floor.

#### **Tuesday: Lower Body (Knee Safe)**

*Focus: Strengthening legs without impact.*
*3 Sets of 12-15 reps.*

1. **Glute Bridges (Weighted):** Lie on back, dumbbell on hips. Thrust hips up, squeezing glutes hard. (Zero knee stress).
2. **Romanian Deadlifts (RDLs):** Holding dumbbells, slight bend in knees, hinge at hips lowering weights toward feet. Feel the stretch in hamstrings. Keep back flat.
3. **Static Reverse Lunges:** Instead of stepping forward (hard on knees), step *back*. If this hurts, switch to **Wall Sits** (hold for 45 sec).
4. **Calf Raises:** Standing, holding weights.
5. **Side Lying Leg Raises:** Lie on side, lift top leg. (Great for hip stability/knee health).

#### **Wednesday: Back & Biceps (Pull) + Obliques**

*Focus: Posture and V-taper.*
*3 Sets of 12-15 reps.*

1. **Bent Over Dumbbell Rows:** Hinge at hips, back flat, pull weights to hip pocket.
2. **Dumbbell Pullovers:** Lie on back (or across a sturdy chair/ottoman), hold one weight with both hands, lower backward over head to stretch lats.
3. **Reverse Flys:** Bent over, light weights, fly arms out like wings (hits rear delts).
4. **Hammer Curls:** Palms facing each other.
5. **Russian Twists:** Seated, leaning back slightly, twist torso side to side (hold a dumbbell for extra challenge).
6. **Bird-Dogs:** On hands and knees, extend opposite arm and leg. Hold for 2 seconds.

#### **Thursday: Low Impact HIIT (Cardio)**

*Focus: Burning calories without running.*
*Format: 40 seconds work, 20 seconds rest. Repeat circuit 4 times.*

1. **Shadow Boxing:** Constant movement, throwing punches (add light dumbbells/water bottles for intensity).
2. **Walkouts (Inchworms):** Stand, hinge at hips, hands to floor, walk hands out to plank, walk back.
3. **Mountain Climbers (Slow & Controlled):** Knee to chest, but do not run. Squeeze the abs.
4. **Step-ups:** Use a low step or stair. Step up, squeeze glute, step down. Alternate legs.
5. **Bicycle Crunches:** Slow tempo.

#### **Friday: Full Body "MetCon"**

*Focus: Endurance and Definition.*
*Complete as many rounds as possible (AMRAP) in 20 minutes.*

* 10 Dumbbell Thrusters (Squat lightly -> Overhead Press)
* 10 Renegade Rows (Plank position holding dumbbells, row one arm then the other)
* 10 Push-ups
* 10 Bodyweight Squats (Limit depth if knee bothers you)
* 20 Second Plank

#### **Saturday: Active Recovery**

* 20-30 minutes of stretching or Yoga.
* Focus on **Quad stretches** and **Hamstring stretches**. Tight quads pull on the knee cap, causing pain. Keeping them loose is vital.

### **Important Notes for Your Knee**

1. **The "No-Go" Zone:** If you feel sharp pain (not muscle burn) in the knee, stop immediately.
2. **Modifications:**
* **Squats:** If regular squats hurt, do **Box Squats**. Sit back onto a chair/couch, pause, and stand up. This forces the hips to do the work, not the knees.
* **Cardio:** If you miss the endurance aspect of running, simple walking at a brisk pace on an incline (if you have a treadmill) or outside is excellent and low stress.

### **Nutrition for Definition**

Since you are 155 lbs and want chest/abs definition, **do not starve yourself**.

* **Protein is King:** To build the chest and abs, you need protein. Aim for ~150g per day.
* **Timing:** Try to eat a protein-rich meal within an hour after these workouts.

Here is a 5-7 minute warm-up specifically designed to "oil the hinges" of your right knee without grinding the gears.

The goal here isn't just to get warm; it is to **activate the stabilizers** (hips, glutes, and ankles) so that your muscles take the load during the workout, not your knee joint.

### **The "Knee-Shield" Warm-Up Routine**

*Do this barefoot or in socks if possible to help strengthen foot stability.*

#### **1. Ankle Mobilization (1 minute)**

*Why: If your ankles are stiff, your knee is forced to twist to compensate.*

* **Ankle Circles:** Lift your right leg slightly. Rotate the ankle slowly in big circles. 15 reps clockwise, 15 reps counter-clockwise. Switch legs.
* **Toe Walks:** Walk on your tiptoes for 15 seconds (wakes up calves).
* **Heel Walks:** Walk on your heels for 15 seconds (wakes up shins/anterior tibialis).

#### **2. Lying Glute Bridges (20 Reps)**

*Why: Running injuries often happen when the glutes "fall asleep" and the knees take over. We need to wake them up first.*

* Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor.
* Drive through your heels to lift your hips.
* **Crucial:** Squeeze your butt hard at the top for 1 second. Do not arch your back.
* Lower slowly.

#### **3. Side-Lying Clamshells (15 Reps per side)**

*Why: This targets the Glute Medius (side hip). When this muscle is weak, the knee caves inward (valgus collapse), causing pain.*

* Lie on your side with legs stacked and knees bent at a 45-degree angle.
* Keep feet touching but open your top knee like a clam shell.
* Keep your hips square (don’t roll backward). You should feel a burn in the side of your hip.

#### **4. Seated Leg Extensions (20 Reps per side)**

*Why: Gently pumps synovial fluid (natural lubricant) into the knee joint without body weight compression.*

* Sit on a chair or the edge of your couch.
* Straighten your right leg slowly until it is fully extended.
* Flex your quad (thigh muscle) hard at the top for 2 seconds.
* Lower slowly.

#### **5. "Towel Squeeze" Mini-Squat (30 Seconds)**

*Why: Engages the VMO (the teardrop muscle on the inside of the knee) which helps the kneecap track correctly.*

* Place a rolled-up towel or small pillow between your knees.
* Stand with feet shoulder-width apart.
* Squeeze the towel hard with your knees.
* While squeezing, perform a *very shallow* squat (only go down 2-3 inches).
* Hold the bottom position for 30 seconds (Isometric hold).

#### **6. Standing Hip Swings (10 Reps per side)**

* Stand next to a wall for balance.
* Swing your outside leg forward and backward gently like a pendulum.
* Do not force the range of motion; just let gravity loosen the hip capsule.

---

### **A Quick "Check Engine" Light**

As you go through this warm-up, listen to your right knee:

* **Clicking/Popping:** Usually fine if there is no pain (just gas escaping the joint).
* **Grinding (Crepitus):** Be cautious. If it feels like "sand in the gears," slow down the movement.
* **Sharp Pain:** If you feel sharp pain during the warm-up, that is a red flag. Skip the leg portion of the workout that day and focus purely on upper body or core.
